# Tyr Vision

> **One Language. Every Digital Architecture.**

---

## Our Mission

Tyr exists to provide a modern hardware description language that enables engineers, researchers, educators, and students to describe digital hardware using a single, consistent language.

Tyr is designed to remain independent of any particular implementation technology, allowing hardware to be described through its behavior and structure rather than the physical medium used to realize it.

---

## Why Tyr?

Digital hardware has evolved through many paradigms:

- Synchronous systems
- Asynchronous systems
- FPGA fabrics
- ASICs
- Binary logic
- Multi-valued logic

Most existing HDLs were designed around the assumptions of their time.

Tyr aims to provide a modern alternative that embraces both established and emerging digital architectures while maintaining a simple and expressive language.

---

## Long-Term Vision

The Tyr Project aims to become a complete open ecosystem for digital hardware development.

This includes:

- The Tyr language
- tyrc compiler
- Language specification
- Standard library
- Official documentation
- IDE tooling
- Reference simulator
- Community-driven ecosystem

---

## Design Philosophy

Tyr follows several fundamental principles.

### Hardware First

The language exists to describe hardware.

Every construct should have a direct hardware interpretation.

---

### Simplicity

Simple syntax is preferred over clever syntax.

The language should be easy to read, reason about, and maintain.

---

### Consistency

The language should behave predictably.

Similar constructs should follow similar rules.

---

### Technology Independence

Tyr should not be tied to any specific hardware implementation.

Backends evolve.

The language remains stable.

---

### Open Development

The Tyr Project is developed in the open.

Major language changes are discussed publicly through RFCs.

---

## The Future

Tyr is intended to become a long-term open standard for digital hardware description.

We believe that hardware description languages should evolve alongside computing itself.

As new architectures emerge, Tyr should continue to provide a common language for describing them.
